Davis-Monthan AFB (309th AMARG) 3D Geospatial Twin

1. Mission Overview

This application is an interactive 3D digital twin comparing sub-meter spaceborne Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) backscatter with true-color optical aerial photography draped over bare-earth LiDAR terrain at Davis-Monthan Air Force Base's 309th Aerospace Maintenance and Regeneration Group (AMARG)—the world's largest military aircraft preservation and reclamation facility in Tucson, Arizona.

Unlike passive optical cameras that rely on sunlight and measure surface color, an active X-band radar satellite transmits its own microwave pulses ($\lambda \approx 3.08\text{ cm}$) and measures the amplitude, phase, and slant-range travel time of the backscattered echo—revealing surface roughness, metallic geometry, and structural height day or night in all weather.

2. Primary Data Attribution & Harmonization Pipeline

SPACEBORNE RADAR
Umbra Space Open Data Program

X-Band Spotlight SAR (Umbra-10, VV polarization, 9.73 GHz, ~25cm impulse / 50cm GEC product, grazing angle ~35° nominal / 60.1° collect), radiometrically calibrated to $\sigma_0$ decibels ($[-25\text{ dB}, +5\text{ dB}]$).

BARE-EARTH ELEVATION
USGS 3DEP 1-Meter LiDAR DTM

Pima County 1-meter Digital Elevation Model (EPSG:32612 / UTM Zone 12N, $807.9\text{ m} - 851.3\text{ m}$ NAVD88), co-registered 1:1 with SAR and optical rasters.

OPTICAL BASEMAP
High-Resolution Public Orthophoto

USGS National Agriculture Imagery Program (NAIP) 30cm true-color RGB aerial orthophoto fused with SAR-derived PBR metalness & surface roughness maps.

3D VECTOR OVERLAYS
OpenStreetMap & Overture Maps

Co-registered hangar footprints, towways, and oriented aircraft storage sectors draped over the displaced 3D terrain mesh with a $+0.2\text{ m}$ elevation offset.

3. Interactive Legend & SAR Scattering Interpretation Guide

Dihedral Double-Bounce Reflections (Saturated Cyan-White / Gold): Right-angled ($90^\circ$) junctions between aircraft fuselages, wing roots, engine nacelles, and the desert floor act as retrodirective corner reflectors. Radar energy bounces twice and returns directly toward the satellite receiver, saturating the upper decibel range ($0\text{ to }+5\text{ dB}$).
Radar Shadow Voids (Pitch-Black Wedges Behind Airframes): Because SAR illuminates the ground obliquely from the side, tall vertical stabilizers (like B-52H and C-5 Galaxy tail fins) block microwave pulses from reaching the ground behind them. The shadow length $L$ directly reveals true structure height via $H = L \cdot \tan(\theta_{\text{grazing}})$.
Specular Ground Scattering (Dark Tarmac & Towways): At X-band wavelength ($3.08\text{ cm}$), paved asphalt taxiways, concrete aprons, and graded caliche hardpan are smoother than the Rayleigh roughness criterion. Incoming radar energy reflects forward away from the sensor like a mirror, making flat pavement appear pitch black ($<-21\text{ dB}$).
